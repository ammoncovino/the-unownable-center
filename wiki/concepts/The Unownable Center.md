# The Unownable Center

The book's title idea: every durable system is built around something it depends on but did not make and cannot finally own.

## Where the manuscript defines it

> [!quote] Preface, line 19
> "every durable system builds itself around a center it did not create and cannot finally own. Empires call that center order. Religions call it God. Markets call it value. Platforms call it engagement. AI labs call it intelligence. But the center remains unownable because it precedes the system, exceeds the system, and outlives the system. Crisis begins when an institution mistakes stewardship for possession and starts treating access to the center as property."

> [!quote] Closing Reflection, line 958
> "The unownable center is the thing a system depends on for legitimacy or survival but cannot rightfully possess: truth, God, reality, the commons, or the future itself."

The two definitions differ in one way worth noticing. The Preface lists what systems *call* the center (order, God, value, engagement, intelligence). The Closing lists what the center *is* (truth, God, reality, the commons, the future). God is the only word on both lists.
