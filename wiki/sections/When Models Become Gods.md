## What it says

- Eden as the moment of accepting that "those consequences are yours."
- Parables as "constructed scenarios," not biography.
- "In my view," later systems stitched many voices into one figure and gave it titles: "Joshua, Jesus, the Christ" (line 246). It adds that a historical teacher need not be denied.
- An argument that an all-knowing, all-powerful, perfectly loving god cannot coherently be the god of the classic heaven and hell picture (line 252).
- "When a model becomes a god, criticism feels like blasphemy."
- "This book does not ask anyone to adopt my conclusion about gods."
- The blunt claim (line 258): a planet that treats religious models as reality "cannot sustain structural peace."

## What it points to
