> [!note] Teaching note: Logos
> Logos is Greek for "word" or "reason." The opening of the Gospel of John ("In the beginning was the Word") uses it, and Christian theology came to identify Jesus with it. The manuscript states this as what "classical Christian language" says, not as its own claim.

## The question for Ammon

The full-prose sections hold the book's frame carefully: the sayings are "attributed," the teacher may be a composite (line 246), and the book "does not attempt to settle" the resurrection. The Closing Reflection, which the recovery notes say was reconstructed and partly synthesized, uses a different register: "Jesus exposes religious and imperial attempts to own the center."

**Whether those two registers sit together, and in whose words the closing stands, is Ammon's to say.** It is listed in [[Open Questions]], B14. This page is not a finding that they conflict.

See also [[The Glass]] for the relayed framing that ties this material to the rest of his work.
